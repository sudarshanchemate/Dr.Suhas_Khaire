Expert Thyroid Care

The thyroid gland controls your metabolism, energy, weight, mood and menstrual cycles. Thyroid problems are common and very treatable, but the dose and the plan need to be right. Dr. Suhas Khaire evaluates your symptoms, blood tests and, where needed, scans, so that treatment is accurate and well monitored.

Thyroid Conditions We Treat

  • Hypothyroidism (underactive thyroid): tiredness, weight gain, feeling cold, hair fall
  • Hyperthyroidism and Graves' disease: palpitations, weight loss, tremor, anxiety
  • Goitre and thyroid nodules (swelling in the neck)
  • Thyroid cancer: hormone management and long-term follow-up after treatment
  • Thyroiditis, including after pregnancy
  • Thyroid disorders in pregnancy and when planning a family
  • Thyroid problems in children and adolescents

How We Manage Thyroid Disorders

  • Interpreting thyroid function tests (TSH, T3, T4) and antibody tests
  • Ultrasound evaluation of nodules or goitre when required
  • Correct medicine dosing, with regular dose review
  • Special care before and during pregnancy
  • Clear guidance on when to test again and how to take your medicines

Frequently Asked Questions

Is thyroid medicine lifelong?

It depends on the cause. Many people with hypothyroidism need long-term treatment, while some forms of thyroiditis or hyperthyroidism are temporary or can go into remission. Your doctor will review this at follow-up visits.

Can I plan a pregnancy if I have a thyroid problem?

Yes. With proper treatment and monitoring, most women with thyroid disorders have healthy pregnancies. Thyroid levels should be checked before conception and regularly during pregnancy, because medicine doses often need to change.

Which tests are needed for thyroid problems?

Usually a TSH test, often along with free T4 and sometimes T3 and thyroid antibodies. An ultrasound may be advised if there is a swelling or nodule.

How should I take my thyroid tablet?

Thyroxine is usually taken on an empty stomach with water, about 30–60 minutes before breakfast, and away from calcium or iron supplements. Follow the specific instructions your doctor gives you.
